# Country Relocation Evaluation Framework

A reusable framework for evaluating countries as potential relocation, second-residency, investment, or long-term “Plan B” destinations. This is written from the perspective of a well-educated American thinking years or decades ahead.

The goal is not to find a perfect country. The goal is to identify places where the weaknesses are knowable, manageable, and compensated by meaningful upside.

---

# How to Use This Framework

Use this framework in three ways:

1. Human thinking tool: understand what matters before choosing countries.
2. LLM prompt context: guide structured country evaluations.
3. Dashboard methodology: explain why the app scores countries the way it does.

This document is not the scoring rubric. For exact 0–10 scoring rules, use `scoring-rubric.md`.

---

# Master Categories

## 1. Legal Residency and Immigration

Can you legally stay there long enough for the country to matter?

Consider:

- Tourist visa length
- Residency pathways
- Digital nomad visas
- Investor visas
- Entrepreneur visas
- Passive-income visas
- Permanent residency
- Citizenship
- Dual citizenship
- Renewal difficulty
- Bureaucratic friction
- Political direction of immigration rules

A country is much less useful as a Plan B if you can only visit temporarily.

---

## 2. Tax System and Wealth Treatment

For Americans, tax analysis must include both local tax rules and U.S. worldwide taxation.

Consider:

- Income tax
- Capital gains tax
- Wealth tax
- Estate tax
- Corporate tax
- VAT/sales tax
- Expat regimes
- Tax treaty with the U.S.
- Foreign bank reporting
- FATCA-related banking issues
- Future risk of tax changes

The ideal country is not always the lowest-tax country. It is a country where taxes are predictable, compliant, and not hostile to foreign residents or capital.

---

## 3. Cost of Living vs U.S.

Cost of living determines quality of life per dollar.

Consider:

- Rent
- Property prices
- Groceries
- Restaurants
- Utilities
- Internet
- Phone plans
- Transportation
- Healthcare
- Imported goods
- Electronics
- Business supplies
- Domestic help

The key question is not “is it cheap?” The key question is “how much good life do I get per dollar?”

---

## 4. Currency and Banking

A country can be attractive but financially dangerous if capital is hard to move or the currency is unstable.

Consider:

- Inflation
- Currency depreciation
- Capital controls
- Parallel exchange rates
- Bank reliability
- Ability for foreigners to open accounts
- Ability for Americans to open accounts
- Wire transfer reliability
- Convertibility
- Deposit insurance
- ATM and card access

A relocation hedge should not trap your money.

---

## 5. Economy and Opportunity

A country may be stable, stagnant, reforming, underpriced, or declining.

Consider:

- Growth
- Inflation
- Debt
- Employment
- Foreign investment
- Reform momentum
- Key industries
- Resource endowment
- Business formation
- Local talent
- Construction and infrastructure growth

The best opportunities often exist where conditions are imperfect but improving.

---

## 6. Employment and Income Portability

The best relocation situation often involves earning globally while living locally.

Consider:

- Remote-work legality
- Time zone compatibility
- Internet reliability
- Local job market
- Local wages
- Business formation
- Payment processors
- Shipping/logistics
- Talent hiring
- English business usability

For Americans with tech, product, or online business skills, income portability can matter more than local wages.

---

## 7. Climate and Climate Change Resilience

Climate strategy is not just nice weather. It is about long-term livability and adaptation.

Consider:

- Heat
- Humidity
- Drought
- Flooding
- Wildfire
- Sea-level rise
- Storm risk
- Water security
- Agriculture
- Grid stress
- Insurance availability
- Government adaptation capacity

A real climate hedge needs water, food, power, infrastructure, and political capacity.

---

## 8. Food, Water, and Energy Security

In bad decades, hard resources matter.

Consider:

- Food production
- Agricultural land
- Fisheries
- Freshwater
- Aquifers
- Rivers
- Hydropower
- Oil/gas
- Nuclear
- Renewables
- Fuel import dependence
- Grid reliability
- Strategic minerals

Countries that can feed and power themselves have more resilience.

---

## 9. Geopolitical Risk

Some beautiful countries sit in dangerous neighborhoods.

Consider:

- Proximity to wars
- Border disputes
- Sanctions risk
- Great-power rivalry
- Military alliances
- Strategic chokepoints
- Trade exposure
- Diplomatic flexibility
- Anti-American sentiment

For a backup base, low geopolitical centrality is often valuable.

---

## 10. Domestic Political Stability

International safety is not enough if the domestic system is chaotic.

Consider:

- Institutional strength
- Policy continuity
- Government turnover
- Corruption
- Court independence
- Protest frequency
- Coup history
- Treatment of foreigners
- Regulatory predictability

Boring institutions are underrated.

---

## 11. Safety and Personal Security

Safety is about whether you can live normally.

Consider:

- Homicide
- Robbery
- Burglary
- Kidnapping
- Organized crime
- Police reliability
- Scams against foreigners
- Emergency response
- Road safety
- Safe neighborhoods

A place can be “safe enough” if risks are predictable and avoidable.

---

## 12. Healthcare

Healthcare matters more over time.

Consider:

- Public healthcare
- Private healthcare
- Insurance cost
- Hospitals
- Specialists
- Emergency care
- Prescriptions
- English-speaking doctors
- Rural vs urban access
- Medical tourism reputation

The best relocation countries often have affordable private care even if the public system is imperfect.

---

## 13. Infrastructure

Infrastructure determines daily friction.

Consider:

- Internet
- Mobile coverage
- Electricity
- Drinking water
- Sewage
- Roads
- Airports
- Ports
- Rail
- Public transit
- Postal/logistics
- Trash collection

Infrastructure is where cheap-country fantasies often break down.

---

## 14. Housing, Land, and Property Rights

Property is only useful if you can own it, protect it, insure it, access it, and sell it.

Consider:

- Foreign ownership rules
- Land rights
- Title reliability
- Squatter risk
- Property tax
- Insurance
- Construction quality
- Liquidity
- Water rights
- Zoning
- Farmland rules

Real estate agents are not neutral sources. Verify property rights carefully.

---

## 15. Demographics

Demographics show whether a country is young, aging, shrinking, growing, importing talent, or losing talent.

Consider:

- Median age
- Fertility
- Population growth
- Immigration
- Emigration
- Brain drain
- Education
- Technical workforce
- Dependency ratio
- Youth optimism

Demographics help distinguish lifestyle destinations from countries with serious future momentum.

---

## 16. Culture, Language, and Social Fit

A strategic country is a bad choice if you hate living there.

Consider:

- English usability
- Local language difficulty
- Attitudes toward Americans
- Expat community
- Friendliness
- Pace of life
- Work culture
- Dating/family culture
- Religion/secular norms
- Food
- Pets
- Hobbies

A good move must work emotionally, not just financially.

---

## 17. Education and Family Suitability

This matters for children, spouses, and long-term household planning.

Consider:

- Public schools
- Private schools
- International schools
- Childcare
- Child safety
- Family healthcare
- Homeschool legality
- Parks
- Family neighborhoods
- Special education

Ask whether you would want to raise a child there during a bad decade.

---

## 18. Transportation and Global Connectivity

A fallback country should not become a trap.

Consider:

- Direct flights to the U.S.
- Flight cost
- Airport quality
- Domestic transport
- Road safety
- Public transit
- Regional mobility
- Emergency exit ability

Remote is good for safety. Too remote can become expensive and isolating.

---

## 19. Technology and Digital Freedom

If income depends on the internet, digital freedom is basic infrastructure.

Consider:

- Internet censorship
- VPN legality
- Surveillance
- Data privacy
- Payment processors
- Cloud access
- Startup ecosystem
- Crypto rules
- Online speech restrictions

A remote worker needs more than fast internet. They need a usable digital legal environment.

---

## 20. Personal Freedom and Civil Liberties

This is subjective but central if the goal is escaping an unbearable political environment.

Consider:

- Speech
- Press freedom
- Protest rights
- Religious freedom
- Privacy
- Police powers
- Emergency powers
- Medical autonomy
- Self-defense laws
- Exit restrictions
- Conscription risk

Some countries are comfortable but controlling. Others are chaotic but freer.

---

## 21. State Capacity

A government can be too weak, too strong, or competent-but-reasonable.

Consider:

- Public administration
- Disaster response
- Public health
- Crime control
- Infrastructure delivery
- Corruption control
- Public services
- Tax collection competence

The sweet spot is competent enough to maintain order but not so intrusive that it crushes private life.

---

## 22. Legal System and Dispute Resolution

Rule of law matters most when something goes wrong.

Consider:

- Courts
- Contract enforcement
- Arbitration
- Corruption
- Lawyer quality
- Police reports
- Foreigner treatment
- Inheritance law
- Marriage/divorce law
- Estate planning

A country can be fun until you have a dispute.

---

## 23. Community and Network Effects

The right people can make a country much more useful.

Consider:

- Expat community
- Entrepreneur community
- Tech community
- Investor community
- Family/homeschool community
- Local networking
- Long-term retention
- Builders vs tourists

Follow the people, but distinguish serious builders from lifestyle tourists.

---

## 24. Quality of Life and Daily Friction

Daily friction determines whether you stay.

Consider:

- Noise
- Pollution
- Cleanliness
- Bureaucracy
- Customer service
- Goods availability
- Repairs
- Delivery
- Walkability
- Nature access
- Fitness
- Restaurants
- Hobbies

Small daily annoyances compound.

---

## 25. Exit Options and Optionality

A Plan B should increase choices, not reduce them.

Consider:

- Ability to leave quickly
- Ability to move money out
- Ability to sell property
- Citizenship path
- Passport value
- Dual citizenship
- Regional mobility
- Business portability

The best relocation decision increases your future moves.

---

## 26. Time Horizon Fit

A country can be good for 6 months, bad for 5 years, and great for 30 years.

Consider:

- Test stay
- Remote-work base
- Family base
- Retirement base
- Climate hedge
- Legacy/property plan

Score countries differently depending on the intended time horizon.

---

## 27. Risk Tolerance Fit

There is no universally best country. There are countries that match different risk profiles.

Consider:

- Stability vs upside
- Bureaucracy tolerance
- Language tolerance
- Currency volatility
- Political volatility
- Crime tolerance
- Infrastructure tolerance
- Frontier-market interest

A high-upside country is often uncomfortable before it becomes obvious.

---

## 28. Foreigner Backlash

Remote workers and wealthy foreigners can become politically unpopular.

Consider:

- Housing backlash
- Foreign buyer restrictions
- Digital nomad resentment
- Anti-Airbnb laws
- Visa tightening
- Anti-American narratives
- Media rhetoric
- Local displacement

A place can be welcoming today and hostile later.

---

## 29. Aging Suitability

A country that works at 35 may not work at 75.

Consider:

- Elder care
- Assisted living
- Home care
- Specialist access
- Prescriptions
- Walkability
- Disability access
- Climate comfort
- Family visitation

Aging suitability is a serious long-term filter.

---

## 30. Supply Chain Resilience

Some countries look cheap until you need specialized goods.

Consider:

- Medication
- Electronics
- Vehicle parts
- Machinery parts
- Business supplies
- Import duties
- Customs delays
- Shipping cost
- Local alternatives

For makers, remote workers, and small business owners, this can matter as much as rent.

---

## 31. Insurance Signals

Insurance markets reveal hidden risk.

Consider:

- Property insurance
- Health insurance
- Disaster coverage
- Car insurance
- Business liability
- Premium trends
- Exclusions
- Claim reliability
- Insurer withdrawal

If insurers avoid a region, ask what they know.

---

## 32. Data Quality and Reality Gap

Online country narratives are often distorted.

Consider:

- Official statistics reliability
- Crime data quality
- Inflation data trustworthiness
- Property listing accuracy
- Expat hype
- Influencer conflicts
- Real estate marketing
- On-the-ground verification

A relocation thesis should be based on reality, not just vibes.

---

## 33. Black Swan and Tail Risk

A country may be fine normally but fragile under stress.

Consider:

- Coup
- Capital controls
- Banking crisis
- Civil unrest
- War spillover
- Natural disaster
- Food/fuel shortages
- Grid failure
- Nationalization
- Anti-foreigner laws

Judge countries by stress-case behavior, not just normal life.

---

## 34. Psychological and Moral Fit

Long-term relocation is not just a spreadsheet decision.

Consider:

- Outsider stress
- Distance from family
- Loneliness
- Language isolation
- Inequality discomfort
- Local contribution
- Personal values
- Identity
- Community

Ask whether you are running toward something or away from something.

---

# Final Thought

The best countries are not the countries with no weaknesses. They are the countries where the weaknesses are visible, manageable, and compensated by real advantages.
